The Right Fit

The Companies That Usually Fit

We work with founder-owned and family-owned manufacturing and industrial businesses that have built real technical capability, strong customer relationships, and a reputation worth protecting.

  • Founder-owned or family-owned, not currently private-equity backed
  • $3 million to $20 million EBITDA
  • $15 million to $150 million revenue
  • Niche manufacturing or industrial services—precision machining, metal forming, stamping, tooling, foundry, aerospace and defense components, machinery, automation equipment, food-processing equipment, industrial textiles, specialty components
  • Real technical capability that is hard to replicate
  • Strong customer relationships built over decades
  • Management team or owner willing to stay involved during a transition
  • Room to grow through people, systems, customers, equipment, or add-on acquisitions

If this sounds close but not exact, it may still be worth a conversation.

The First Call

No Materials. No Obligation. No Decision to Sell.

A first conversation is 20 minutes. It is private. You do not need to send financials, customer lists, confidential information memorandums, or any other materials.

The call is simply to understand:

  • What you built
  • What you are worried about
  • Whether the company fits any serious buyer or capital partner
  • What a buyer would likely care about
  • Whether staying independent, bringing in a partner, or doing nothing may be the right answer

The first call is not a process. It is a conversation.

What Thoughtful Partners Offer

Taking Risk Off the Table Without Walking Away

In some transactions, an owner sells part of the company and keeps ownership in the next version of the business. That structure can allow you to take some risk off the table while still participating if the company grows.

Continuity

The owner may remain involved. Management may continue running the company. Employees and customers may see continuity, not disruption.

Capital for Growth

The company may gain capital for equipment, acquisitions, new facilities, or expansion—without taking on traditional bank debt.

Future Upside

With rollover equity, the owner may participate in future growth. If the company doubles in value, the owner's remaining stake grows with it.

Structure matters. Buyer quality matters. Legal, tax, accounting, and financial advice matter. Nothing should be rushed.
